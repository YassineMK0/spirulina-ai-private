# Spirulina Cultivation Troubleshooting Guide

## Color Problems

### Culture turns bright green instead of blue-green
**Cause**: Chlorella or green algae contamination. Spirulina is blue-green (cyan) due to phycocyanin. Bright green coloration indicates the culture is being outcompeted by green microalgae such as Chlorella vulgaris.
**Diagnosis**: Observe under microscope — spirulina filaments (helix shape) should dominate. Green round cells = Chlorella.
**Solution**:
- Raise pH above 10.5 for 24-48 hours (Chlorella cannot survive above pH 10.5, spirulina tolerates it)
- Filter the culture through a fine sieve (50-80 micron mesh) to remove contaminating cells
- Dilute with fresh sterile medium to reduce contaminant density
- Ensure agitation is consistent — dead zones promote Chlorella growth

### Culture turns yellow or pale
**Cause**: Nitrogen (azote) deficiency. Spirulina loses its blue-green color when nitrogen-starved, as it degrades phycocyanin (a nitrogen-containing pigment) for energy.
**Diagnosis**: Check EC and nitrate levels. Yellow coloration with no smell = nutrient deficiency, not contamination.
**Solution**:
- Add sodium nitrate (NaNO3) or urea to restore nitrogen levels
- Check electrical conductivity (conductivité) — if EC is low, full medium replenishment is needed
- Never let nitrogen be depleted; monitor EC and color daily (quotidien)

### Culture turns brown or red
**Cause**: Iron deficiency or oxidative stress. Can also indicate excessive light (photoinhibition) or temperature shock.
**Solution**:
- Add ferrous sulfate (FeSO4) to restore iron
- Check if culture was exposed to direct midday sun without shading
- Brown coloration after temperature crash may indicate irreversible culture damage

## Odor Problems

### Culture smells bad, sulfurous, or rotten
**Cause**: Bacterial contamination (bactéries). Heterotrophic bacteria produce hydrogen sulfide and organic acids causing sulfurous odor.
**Diagnosis**: Good spirulina culture has a mild earthy/grassy smell. Any sulfurous, fishy, or putrid odor = bacterial overgrowth.
**Risk factors**: Organic contamination from poorly cleaned equipment, dead spirulina cells decomposing, pH dropping below 8.
**Solution**:
- Remove contaminated culture volume (30-50%)
- Raise pH to 10.0+ to inhibit bacterial growth (most heterotrophic bacteria cannot survive above pH 9.5)
- Disinfect all equipment with 5% bleach solution, rinse thoroughly
- Replace with fresh medium and restart from a clean inoculum if contamination is severe

## Growth Problems

### Culture crash (effondrement)
**Causes of sudden culture collapse**:
1. pH exceeding 11.5 — irreversible membrane damage to spirulina cells
2. Temperature below 15°C or above 40°C for more than 2 hours
3. Severe contamination by predators (ciliates, rotifers) or bacteria
4. Sudden medium dilution with cold water (thermal shock)
5. Complete nutrient exhaustion (EC below 10 mS/cm)
6. Power failure stopping agitation — culture settles, oxygen depletes, cells die

**Prevention**:
- Monitor pH twice daily — never let it exceed 11.3
- Monitor temperature continuously — maintain 30-38°C
- Check EC weekly — replenish medium before depletion
- Always pre-warm water to culture temperature before adding

**Recovery after partial crash**:
- Remove 50% of damaged culture
- Refill with fresh pre-warmed medium (même température)
- Maintain at low light (half shading) for 2-3 days
- Do not harvest during recovery phase
- If >80% of culture is damaged, restart from preserved inoculum

### Slow growth (3+ days)
**Diagnosis sequence** (in order):
1. Check EC — if below target range, nutrients are limiting
2. Check pH — if above 10.8, CO2 limitation is slowing photosynthesis
3. Check temperature — below 25°C significantly reduces growth rate
4. Check light — insufficient light or too much shading
5. Check culture density — if OD680 > 1.5, self-shading is limiting growth

### OD680 drop (chute soudaine)
**Causes**:
- Over-harvesting: removed too large a fraction at once
- Dilution with excess water during medium change (renouvellement)
- Temperature shock from cold water addition
- Beginning of culture crash (check for color change and smell)
- Sensor contamination — clean probe before re-reading

## pH Problems

### pH keeps dropping below 8.5
**Cause**: Insufficient bicarbonate (bicarbonate) — the carbon source for photosynthesis.
**Mechanism**: Spirulina consumes CO2/bicarbonate during photosynthesis. Without replenishment, pH drops as CO2 accumulates from respiration.
**Solution**:
- Add sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3) — the primary carbon source and pH buffer
- Check if culture is receiving adequate light — low light = less photosynthesis = less bicarbonate consumption
- Increase aeration to remove excess CO2
- Target: maintain pH between 8.5 and 10.5; optimum around 9.5

### pH too high (above 11)
**Cause**: Excessive photosynthesis consuming CO2 faster than it can be replenished — common on bright sunny afternoons.
**Solution**:
- Increase aeration to off-gas excess CO2
- Add shading (demi-ombrage) to reduce photosynthesis rate
- Add CO2 if available
- Never add acid (HCl) unless absolutely necessary — sudden pH swings are dangerous

## Foaming (Mousse)

### Excessive foaming
**Causes**:
- Organic contamination: dead cells or bacteria releasing surfactant proteins
- Over-agitation: too high paddlewheel speed or air pressure breaking surface tension
- Drying spray from harvesting equipment contaminating the pond surface

**Solution**:
- Reduce agitation speed temporarily
- Remove foam manually with a clean paddle
- If organic smell accompanies foam, treat as bacterial contamination
- A small amount of foam at the edges is normal in active cultures

## Contamination Management

### Detecting contamination early
**Daily visual checks** (quotidien):
- Color should be homogeneous blue-green
- No clumping or settling of filaments
- No unusual odor
- No visible foam or scum

**Weekly microscope check**:
- Sample 1 mL of culture
- Observe at 100-400x magnification
- Spirulina filaments should be >90% of microorganisms
- Look for: Chlorella (round green cells), rotifers (moving predators), filamentous fungi

### Recovering from partial contamination (contamination partielle)
1. **Filter** through 80-micron sieve to remove large contaminants
2. **Dilute**: replace 30-50% of contaminated culture with fresh sterile medium (milieu)
3. **Raise pH** to 10.0-10.5 to inhibit non-alkalophilic contaminants
4. **Monitor**: check daily for 5 days, remove contaminated fractions if needed
5. **Harvest** smaller amounts until culture is healthy again

### Chlorella contamination (contamination Chlorella)
Chlorella is the most common spirulina contaminant.
- **pH method**: maintain pH 10.5+ for 48 hours (Chlorella dies, spirulina survives)
- **Filtration**: use 50-micron filter — Chlorella (3-5 µm) passes through while spirulina filaments (50-500 µm) are retained
- **Prevention**: disinfect all water sources, prevent rain contamination, cover ponds

## Harvest Operations

### Best time of day to harvest (matin — morning)
Harvest in the **early morning** (6-9 AM) before the pH peaks:
- Culture density is highest after overnight growth
- pH is at its daily minimum (8.5-9.5) — avoids harvesting during pH spike
- Temperature is lower — better biomass quality and color retention
- Phycocyanin content is highest in the morning

### How much to harvest (calcul de la récolte)
**Safe harvest volume** = Total volume × (1 - Target_OD/Current_OD)
- Never harvest more than 30% of culture volume in a single day
- Target OD680: maintain between 0.4 and 1.0 for optimal growth rate
- High-density cultures (OD > 1.5) grow slower due to self-shading — harvest more frequently

### Standard harvest procedure
1. Stop agitation 15-30 minutes before harvest (allows partial settling)
2. Collect culture from the highest-density zone
3. Filter through harvest filter (sieve: 50-80 microns)
4. Press biomass gently to remove excess water
5. Dry immediately at 40-65°C maximum (higher temperatures damage phycocyanin)
6. Store dried biomass in airtight, light-proof containers

### Drying spirulina biomass (séchage)
- **Temperature limit**: never exceed 65°C — phycocyanin degrades above 65°C
- **Recommended**: 40-50°C forced air drying or solar drying with shading
- **Solar drying**: spread in thin layers (2-3 mm), cover with UV-transparent film to protect from insects
- Target: < 8% moisture content for storage stability

## Routine Monitoring (Paramètres quotidiens)

**Daily measurements**:
- pH (morning and afternoon)
- Temperature
- OD680 or visual density check

**Weekly measurements**:
- Electrical conductivity (EC / conductivité électrique) — mS/cm
- Microscopy check for contamination
- Harvest calculation

**Target ranges**:
- pH: 8.5 – 10.5 (optimum: 9.5)
- Temperature: 30 – 38°C (optimum: 35°C)
- EC: 20 – 35 mS/cm
- OD680: 0.4 – 1.2 (harvest when > 0.8)

---

## Medium Renewal (Renouvellement du milieu)

### Partial medium change
- Replace 10-20% of culture volume with fresh medium weekly
- Always pre-warm water to culture temperature before adding
- Add nutrients proportional to replacement volume
- After harvesting, refill to original volume with fresh medium

### Full medium change
- Perform every 3-6 months or when EC cannot be maintained despite nutrient addition
- Filter culture, discard old medium, restart with fresh Zarrouk or equivalent
- Keep 10-20% of old culture as inoculum

---

## Scale-Up (Agrandissement)

### From 100L to 1000L
1. **Inoculum preparation**: grow starter culture to OD680 > 0.8 in small volume (inoculum sain)
2. **Inoculation density**: target OD680 = 0.3-0.4 in the new pond
3. **Inoculation volume**: inoculum volume = (Target_OD / Current_OD) × New_volume
4. **Gradual scaling**: 100L → 300L → 1000L (don't jump more than 5x in one step)
5. **Medium**: prepare full Zarrouk medium at correct EC (20-25 mS/cm) for new volume
6. **First harvest**: wait until OD680 > 0.8 before first harvest (7-14 days typical)

---

## Storage During Production Break (Stockage)

### Short break (1-2 weeks)
- Reduce culture to 20-30% of normal volume
- Move to indoor low-light conditions (500-1000 lux)
- Reduce temperature to 20-25°C (slows metabolism)
- No harvesting during break
- Check pH and EC every 3 days; add a small bicarbonate dose if pH drops below 8.5

### Long break (1-3 months — arrêt prolongé)
- Concentrate culture to very high density (OD680 > 2.0)
- Store in refrigerator (4-8°C) — culture enters dormancy
- Sealed container, light-protected
- To restart: bring back to room temperature over 24 hours, dilute and expose to light gradually
- Full production density recovers in 2-3 weeks

### Emergency storage
- Dry a small batch of live spirulina paste (not fully dried — 30-40% moisture)
- Freeze at -18°C
- Can revive after 6-12 months; use as inoculum
